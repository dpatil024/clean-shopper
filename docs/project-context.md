## 1. Problem Statement
People want to make more informed purchasing decisions for their home and family — replacing toxic cleaning products, finding personal care items without harmful chemicals, or stocking a pantry with cleaner food options — but researching ingredient safety and verifying "clean" or "natural" claims is overwhelming, expertise-dependent, and time-consuming.

## 2. ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)
Someone actively trying to replace toxic cleaning products, find personal care items without harmful chemicals, or build a cleaner pantry. They currently research alternatives across multiple fragmented sources and have accumulated preferences over time — trusted brands, ingredients they avoid — but have no place to store or apply those preferences consistently. They interact with the product through a conversational interface backed by Claude, rather than browsing or filtering a catalog.

## 3. Pain Points
- Product labels are confusing and ingredient lists require expertise to interpret
- "Clean" and "natural" claims on packaging are often misleading or unverified
- Researching alternatives across multiple sources is fragmented and slow
- Preferences built up over time — trusted brands, avoided ingredients — have nowhere to live

## 4. Proposed Solution
- Describe what they're looking for and receive researched product recommendations with reasoning, evaluated against clean standards
- Save preferences: ingredients to avoid, trusted brands, and certifications that matter to them (e.g. EWG Verified, USDA Organic, B Corp)
- Have saved preferences applied automatically to every subsequent recommendation
- Add recommended products to a shopping cart that persists across sessions
- Compare products side by side and get a clear recommendation based on saved preferences

## 6. Design Constraints
Platform: Conversational interface backed by Claude
Geography: Not defined in source material
Accessibility: Not defined in source material
Technical: Claude searches the web in real time for product research; draws on ingredient safety databases such as EWG's Skin Deep
Other: Out of scope for V1 — checkout or payment processing, direct retailer integrations, barcode scanning, user accounts or authentication, mobile app. This is the course demo project for "Claude Code for Designers," built over four weeks by students, with the instructor demonstrating the full workflow live in each session.

## 7. Open Questions
1. What counts as "clean" when evaluating a product — how are ingredient safety data, certifications, and sustainability criteria weighted or combined into a single recommendation?
2. Since V1 has no user accounts, how should "persists across sessions" (preferences, cart) actually be implemented?
3. What is the intended scope of real-time web search for product research (which retailers/sources, how results are ranked)?

## 8. Gaps
1. **Success Metrics** — the brief does not define what a successful outcome looks like or how it would be measured. Needed before V1 can be evaluated as working.
2. **Geography** — no stated launch region or locale restrictions, which affects which retailers, certifications, and safety databases are relevant.
3. **Accessibility** — no stated accessibility requirements, which should be defined before UI design work begins.
4. **Technical/platform detail** — the brief does not specify frontend framework, data layer, hosting, or styling approach.
5. **ICP specificity** — the target user is described by motivation and frustration, not by concrete behavioral detail (e.g. shopping frequency, current tools/apps used, budget sensitivity), which would sharpen design decisions.
